## 1. Executive Summary & Purpose

Macro- and micro-encapsulation of stem cell-derived insulin-producing cells represent a frontline strategy for treating Type 1 Diabetes (T1D) without systemic immunosuppression. However, preclinical studies frequently encounter graft failure due to unexpected physical limitations (hypoxia, fibrotic capsule growth, and acute IBMIR-mediated cell loss). 

This brief summarizes key computational recommendations generated by our literature-calibrated digital twin engine (`t1d_simulator`). It outlines specific design parameters for geometry, seeding density, surface functionalization, and transplantation site selection to maximize graft survival and functional insulin independence.

## 2. Quantitative Failure Mode Recommendations

### 2.1. Geometry & The Krogh Diffusion Limit
- **Slab Hydrogel Macroencapsulation**: Planar hydrogel sheets must not exceed **$L = 150\ \mu\text{m}$ in half-thickness** ($300\ \mu\text{m}$ total thickness). Slabs exceeding $200\ \mu\text{m}$ develop severe central core hypoxia ($pO_2 < 0.5\ \text{mmHg}$) under physiological subcutaneous conditions ($pO_2 = 30\ \text{mmHg}$).
- **Spherical Microcapsules**: Microcapsules of radius $R = 250–350\ \mu\text{m}$ offer a favorable surface-area-to-volume ratio ($3/R$). Microcapsules exceeding $R = 400\ \mu\text{m}$ experience rapid core necrosis at cell packing densities $>50\ \text{M/ml}$.
- **TPMS Gyroid Scaffolds**: Triply Periodic Minimal Surface (TPMS) Gyroid architectures with pore sizes of **$200–250\ \mu\text{m}$** optimize fluid perfusion while preventing structural collapse.

### 2.2. Seeding Density Limits
- **Recommended Range**: **$30–60\ \text{million cells/ml}$** of hydrogel matrix.
- **Hypoxia Threshold**: Cell densities exceeding $80\ \text{M/ml}$ trigger excessive oxygen consumption ($V_{max} \ge 1.5 \times 10^{-16}\ \text{mol}/(\text{cell} \cdot \text{s})$), accelerating $H_2O_2$ ROS accumulation and causing $>50\%$ viability loss within 72 hours in non-vascularized sites.

## 3. Surface Engineering & Anti-IBMIR Interventions

### 3.1. Lipid-PEG-LMWH Nanocoating
- Direct cell surface modification with amphiphilic Lipid-PEG-Low Molecular Weight Heparin (LMWH) suppresses Tissue Factor (CD142)-mediated thrombin generation.
- **In Silico Finding**: A surface coating density of $\ge 1.0\ \mu\text{g/cm}^2$ reduces 48-hour IBMIR cell loss from $35–60\%$ down to **$<10\%$**.

### 3.2. Zwitterionic Hydrogel Coatings (SBAA / CBAA)
- Encapsulation hydrogels coated with sulfobetaine acrylamide (SBAA) or carboxybetaine acrylamide (CBAA) attenuate non-specific protein adsorption.
- **GNN Model Prediction**: Zwitterionic hydrogel modification maintains fibrotic capsule thickness $L_{fib} < 20\ \mu\text{m}$ over 60 days in vivo, preserving oxygen diffusion flux.

## 4. Anatomical Implantation Site Selection

Computational screening across 8 physiological and clinical criteria yields the following site hierarchy:

1. **Omental Pouch (Pre-Vascularized Greater Omentum)** — **Rank 1 (Score: 92/100)**:
   - High baseline oxygenation ($pO_2 \approx 55\ \text{mmHg}$).
   - $100\%$ surgical retrievability in case of adverse events or $iCasp9$ suicide switch activation.
   - Low IBMIR risk ($10\%$) compared to intravascular infusion.
2. **Direct Arterial / AV-Loop** — **Rank 2 (Score: 78/100)**:
   - Near-arterial $pO_2$ ($95\ \text{mmHg}$), enabling high cell packing densities ($>100\ \text{M/ml}$).
   - Requires localized anticoagulation to prevent thrombosis.
3. **Intraportal Vein (Clinical Baseline)** — **Rank 3 (Score: 55/100)**:
   - Severe IBMIR exposure ($100\%$) and non-retrievable graft placement.
4. **Subcutaneous (SQ)** — **Rank 4 (Score: 45/100)**:
   - Severe baseline hypoxia ($pO_2 = 30\ \text{mmHg}$) requiring co-delivery of angiogenic factors (VEGF/PDGF).
